## What changed since the last handoff

**Play mode makes sound.** Spec: `specs/2026-10-14-play-mode-sound-design.md`.

- A trick is a **read head** that leaves the transport while a pad is held.
  The transport keeps counting underneath, so a release is back in time with
  nothing resynchronised. The MIDI clock out, catches and punches never see a
  trick.
- The pads:

  | Pad | Sound |
  |---|---|
  | BREAK | Silence |
  | STOP | Winds down over a beat |
  | REV | Backwards |
  | CHOP | The last slice, its length from the quantise switch |
  | ½× / 2× | Varispeed |
  | 1–4 | Drop that track out |

- How a press travels:
  - The page sends each press and release over `GET /api/tapes/play` the
    moment it happens, and the Pi quantises it on its render head.
  - The Pi reports its **lead**, how long a press takes to be heard. It
    measures 225 ms on the main rig.
  - The page draws the lead as the gap between a pad going dim and going
    bright.
- Code:
  - `internal/tape/trick.go` and `internal/api/play.go`;
  - `web/static/lib/play/link.js`, with `clock.js` for the grid and the ring.
- An independent review found six defects in the engine before merge, all
  fixed with failing-first tests. The worst was a tick at every loop seam on
  loop-filling clips.
- **A jam is already recorded.** The tape's output comes back through the
  Sidekick's MAIN, so tricks played are in the ring and save as a take like
  anything else.

**The object library is finished** (#98). These now live in `lib/objects/`:
- the VU meter, reels, reel window, cassette, ribbon, LCD, grease pencil,
  pennant and icons;
- the label as materials, with no module.

The transport keys were left out: they are the app's buttons, styled by
tokens that follow the scheme. The bench at `/objects.html` has a size slider
and a PNG still.

**Releases are now an allow-list** (#97). Only `cmd/`, `internal/`, `web/`,
`deploy/` (less the hub's manifests), `go.mod`/`go.sum` and `docs/guide.md`
cut one. Docs like this note don't.

**Two Play mode fixes reached every kit:**

## Next: the design session

Gabe wants the play controls **tightly integrated with the interface**,
worked out with design tools. Above all he wants **the OP-1's workflow to
feel intuitive**: the way the tape is used, more than any one control.

**The workflow comes first.** Hindsight already has every step. What it
lacks is a path through them that needs no thought:

| Step | What happens | Where it lives now |
|---|---|---|
| **Catch** | You just played something good. It's already in the ring, so the last N bars go onto a track, or a free loop is tapped | Capture page, the tape's Catch |
| **Layer** | Overdub onto the other tracks, or catch more passes | Tape page: arm a track, punch, Catch |
| **Perform** | Hold tricks and track keys over the loop | Play mode, behind a flag and a button |
| **Keep** | The dashcam recorded the performance (the tape's output comes back through MAIN), so it saves as a take | Capture page; or a mixdown |

The question for the boards: from "I just played something good" to "it's
looping, I'm performing over it, and it's kept", how few touches, on a
phone and on a laptop? What is on screen at each step, and what carries you
to the next? Storyboard that loop first; the controls follow from it.

Then the details:

1. **Overlay or integrated?** Play mode is a full-screen overlay today (the
   play-mode spec's decision 2). "Tight integration" may mean the pads belong
   on the tape page itself, near the deck, rather than behind a PLAY button.
   Start by deciding this, with boards.
2. **The picture reacts to the sound.** Put the reel window from
   `lib/objects` on the surface, moved by the shared ticker and
   `motion.spin()`:
   - STOP winds it down with inertia over the beat;
   - REV runs it backwards;
   - ½× and 2× change its speed;
   - CHOP rocks it;
   - BREAK keeps it turning while the sound cuts.

   It moves on the Pi's `at` message, when the sound does. The pad's dim
   state answers the touch at once.
3. **A way in that can't be missed.** On a phone the floating PLAY button
   sits over the Tape tab, and Gabe didn't see it. Proposed: a Play key in
   the deck beside ▶ and Catch, shown only with the flag.
4. **App-wide motion**, already agreed in the object-library spec:
   - reels spin up and coast down;
   - tape slack on a stop;
   - transport keys with travel;
   - one shared ticker.
5. **One object upgrade to prove the loop:** a more realistic cassette shell,
   judged on the bench.

Constraints that are already decided:

- **Hindsight is the tape, not the voice.** A pad lands on a grid line,
  about a quarter second after the hand. Nothing should need sound to follow
  a finger within about 20 ms; that belongs to the instruments in the room.
  See the latency table in the sound spec.
- **Surfaces are drawn in materials**, the same in both schemes: the `--pad-*`
  tokens in `objects.css`.
- **Behind the per-device flag** until Gabe says otherwise.
- **Look at it in a browser before it merges.** Use headless Playwright: the
  Playwright MCP browser opens windows on Gabe's screen.

The Reel-to-reel board generators live outside this repo, on Gabe's
machine. Ask him for them if new boards should match the shipped ones.

## How the sound was checked, for whoever changes it next

There's no ear in a sandbox, so:
- `trick_test.go` reads a ramp-signal tape, so every output value says where
  the head read. It includes:
  - continuity checks (the biggest step between two frames);
  - a loop-filling clip played through its seam;
  - a 300-trial random jam;
  - a two-engine test that a jam leaves the tape sample-identical once let go.
- End to end:
  1. Put the demo's output in `both` (`PUT /api/tapes/output`).
  2. Record `/api/tapes/stream` (stamped PCM16 packets) while a script holds
     pads on `/api/tapes/play`.
  3. Read the level per 50 ms. BREAK reads 0 on its sixteenth, and STOP
     falls to 0 over a beat.
- `BenchmarkRenderABlock` is the CPU number to re-run on a Pi: one 21 ms block
  with four tracks, plain and under each trick.
